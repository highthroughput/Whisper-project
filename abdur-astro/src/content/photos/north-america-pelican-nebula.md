The North America and Pelican Nebulae are two parts of the same glowing
hydrogen cloud in Cygnus, near the bright star Deneb. A dark dust lane
splits the cloud so that one side looks like the continent of North America
and the other like a pelican. They lie about 2,600 light-years away. The
North America Nebula alone spans roughly 90 light-years and covers about
two degrees of sky: four times the width of the full Moon. Hot young stars
ionize the gas and make it shine red in hydrogen-alpha, while new stars are
still forming in the dust along the Cygnus Wall.
