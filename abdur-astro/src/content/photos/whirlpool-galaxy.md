A grand-design spiral caught mid-collision. The little companion, NGC 5195, has been
swinging past the Whirlpool for a few hundred million years, and the encounter is what
winds those arms so tightly and lights them up with star formation. At 31 million
light-years it is one of the few galaxies where a backyard telescope resolves real
structure — dust lanes, H II regions, the bridge of material between the pair.

Eleven clear nights in an Alberta spring is a gift; most of the luminance came in two
long moonless stretches. Processed for a natural galaxy palette: no narrowband, just
careful LRGB with the core held back so the dust lanes stay legible all the way in.
