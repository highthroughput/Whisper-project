Ten thousand years ago a star in Cygnus let go, and this is the wreckage: a shock wave
still ploughing into the interstellar medium at hundreds of kilometres a second. The
red filaments are hydrogen, the teal ones doubly-ionized oxygen — the two trace
different temperatures in the same blast wave, which is why they interleave instead of
overlapping.

A pure two-filter image. OIII is fragile — it lives in the same part of the spectrum
as light pollution — so the whole oxygen stack came from Bortle 2 skies near Nordegg,
where the Milky Way casts shadows and the loudest thing all night is the coyotes.
