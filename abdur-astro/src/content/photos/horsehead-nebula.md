The most recognizable dark nebula in the sky is not an object so much as an absence —
a cold column of dust a lightyear tall, blocking the glowing curtain of IC 434 behind
it. Give it five million years and the same radiation sculpting it will have erased it.

Hydrogen-alpha does all the heavy lifting here: eleven hours of it, shot through
January moonlight that would have ruined a broadband image. A few short RGB hours on
a dark weekend put natural colour back into the stars.
