The Rosette Nebula is a winter emission nebula in Monoceros. It forms a
wreath of ionized hydrogen around the young cluster NGC 2244. The bright
ultraviolet light and winds from the stars have carved a cavity in the
middle of the glowing gas cloud. It sits about 5,200 light-years away and
spans roughly 130 light-years, so on the sky it is about 1.3° across: two
and a half times the width of the full Moon.
