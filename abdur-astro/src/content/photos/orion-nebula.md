The brightest nebula in the northern sky, and still the hardest to do justice. The
Trapezium core is thousands of times brighter than the outer wings, so this is really
two images: short exposures to hold the four newborn stars at the centre, long ones to
reach the faint outer shell, blended with high-dynamic-range masking so neither lies.

Shot through the coldest week of the year — the camera barely needed its cooler at
−32 °C outside. Narrowband through the winter moon, with the palette mapped back
toward the hydrogen reds the eye would never see but the sky actually emits.
