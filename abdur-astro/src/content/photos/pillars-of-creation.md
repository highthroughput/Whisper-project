The most famous four light-years in astronomy, shot from a foothills clearing west of
Nordegg. The pillars are being eaten and lit at the same time — ultraviolet radiation
from the young cluster above them erodes the dust while making its edges fluoresce.
Everything you see here is a slow-motion demolition, a few hundred thousand years from
being gone.

Summer imaging at 52° north is a race: real darkness lasts barely three hours in June.
Nine short nights, meridian flips timed to the minute, and the full Hubble-style
sulfur–hydrogen–oxygen mapping in return. Worth every mosquito.
