One star did this. SAO 20575 is forty-five times the mass of the Sun, and its stellar
wind — a few million kilometres per hour of it — has inflated a soap bubble seven
light-years across in the surrounding gas. The star isn't even at the centre; it's
offset toward the top, ploughing through denser material on one side.

Cassiopeia rides high over Alberta all autumn, so this field crosses the zenith where
the air is thinnest. Ten October nights of narrowband, processed to keep the bubble's
rim crisp without letting the sulfur channel turn the whole field to rust.
