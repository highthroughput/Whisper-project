On July 4, 1054, Chinese astronomers logged a "guest star" bright enough to see in
daylight. This is what it looks like 970 years later: a cloud of shredded star nine
light-years across, still expanding at 1,500 km/s, with a pulsar at the centre spinning
thirty times a second and powering the eerie glow between the filaments.

M 1 is small — even at 1,480 mm it's a compact target — so this one leans on seeing.
The best frames came on two still November nights when the jet stream moved off and
the stars stopped boiling. Sometimes Alberta's winter high-pressure systems pay rent.
