One of the closest and most recognizable star clusters in the sky, visible to the
naked eye from almost anywhere. The blue haze wrapped around the brightest stars
isn't leftover material from their birth: it's an unrelated cloud of interstellar
dust the cluster happens to be passing through right now, lit up by starlight
passing through it.

At roughly 444 light-years, Pleiades is close enough that even a modest telescope
resolves the individual stars distinctly, each one wrapped in its own wisp of blue.
