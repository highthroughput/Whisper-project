Everyone has seen the Orion Nebula; almost no one has seen it twenty hours deep. The
famous bright core is here, but so is everything the quick versions miss — the faint
outer shell, the dark bay, the dust curtains that frame the whole cavern. It is the
piece guests ask about.

The reds in this image are real emission — hydrogen glowing at its own wavelength —
and they carry remarkably well in giclée. On baryta fibre the core takes on the slight
sheen of a darkroom print, which suits the subject: this is the object that got
astrophotography started in 1880.
