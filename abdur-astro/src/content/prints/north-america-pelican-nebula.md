Two of the sky's largest emission nebulae, sitting side by side in Cygnus and
separated only by a lane of dark dust that blocks the hydrogen glow behind it. The
larger cloud's outline is close enough to the coastline of North America that the
name stuck; its neighbour is shaped enough like a pelican's head and beak to earn
its own.

Both are part of the same star-forming region, roughly two thousand light-years
away, lit by a hot young star hidden in the dust nearby.
