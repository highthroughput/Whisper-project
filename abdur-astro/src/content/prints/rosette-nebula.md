A ring of glowing hydrogen roughly a hundred light-years across, with its centre
cleared out by the radiation and stellar wind of the young star cluster still
embedded at its heart. The nebula and its cluster sit about 5,200 light-years away
in Monoceros.

The shape that gives it its name only really appears in a wide, deep exposure: up
close, the ring resolves into knots and filaments of gas still collapsing into new
stars.
