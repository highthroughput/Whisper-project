# Writing rules for this site

This applies to every piece of visitor-facing text on abdurastro.com: page
copy, meta titles and descriptions, alt text, taglines, button labels,
form labels and confirmation messages, JSON-LD fields, email subject
lines generated by forms, and anything else a visitor (or a search
engine, or an email inbox) will see.

## No em dashes, ever

Never use an em dash (—) anywhere in site content. Rewrite the sentence
instead: split it into two sentences, or use a comma, colon, semicolon,
or "and"/"but" depending on the relationship between the clauses.

For a structural separator (page titles, JSON-LD names, generated email
subjects) use the middle dot `·` that the design already uses elsewhere
(e.g. "Field Station · 52.3° N", "Observation № 01 · Constellation"),
not a dash of any kind.

This rule doesn't extend to code comments, commit messages, or this file
itself — only to text a visitor can actually see or read.

## Don't write like an AI

Beyond em dashes, avoid the patterns that make copy read as AI-generated:

- No "It's not just X, it's Y" or "more than just a photograph" framing.
- No stacked rule-of-three adjectives ("stunning, breathtaking, awe-inspiring").
- No hedge-y throat-clearing ("It's worth noting that...", "In today's world...").
- No exclamation points in body copy (fine in a real quoted video title).
- Keep sentences the length a person would actually write: some short,
  some long, not a metronome of medium ones.
- When quoting an external title (a YouTube video title, etc.), match the
  real title exactly, including its actual punctuation. Don't "upgrade"
  a plain hyphen in a real title to an em dash.
